# Subject and age progression

These are adaptable teaching suggestions, not publisher certification or a
verified mapping to a national curriculum. Preserve the brief and seek educator
review; use no copied book pages, characters, dialogue or lyrics.

Pre-K ages 3–4: sensory comparison, oral naming, large motor paths; one or two
short activities. KG1 ages 4–5: sorting, matching, guided tracing and concrete
counting. KG2 ages 5–6: prediction, simple recording, phoneme blending and
guided explanation. Primary: worked examples, guided/independent practice and
review. Handwriting row examples: 22/20/18 mm, 14 mm in early primary; these are
adjustable design choices rather than universal age standards.

## Science
Suggested topics: senses and safe observation; living/non-living; plant growth
and needs; animals and habitats; materials, floating, push/pull and magnets;
weather, day/night and care for the environment. Sequence observation → oral
prediction → safe adult-supervised test → picture/table record → comparison.
Do not assume all living things require sunlight or that all seeds germinate in
the same conditions. Avoid tasting unknown objects and unsafe sensory tasks.
Rotate scene discovery, labelled diagram, sequencing strip, sorting mat,
comparison spread, experiment journal, partner discussion and cumulative recap.

## Mathematics
Move concrete objects → pictures → symbols. Select ranges from the brief and
actual readiness: quantities 1–5, then 0–10 and 11–20; sorting, patterns,
position, shapes, measure, picture graphs and simple operations. Use five/ten
frames, number lines and part/whole diagrams with native shapes and text.
Check quantity-to-label agreement and every answer. Keep learner answer zones
empty unless the page is an explicitly worked example.

## Arabic and reading
Oral vocabulary, pencil control, letter recognition, contextual forms, sound
segmentation/blending and meaningful short reading. Use natural Unicode RTL.
Only add instructional diacritics required for the reading objective. Distinguish
letter names from sounds and verify examples and joining forms with an educator.

## English and phonics
Phonemic listening, selected sound-symbol sequence, blending and short original
texts. Use handwriting guides, phoneme boxes and separate LTR labels. Choose
available legible fonts; do not assume a font is free, installed or licensed.

## Values, Islamic studies and enrichment
Use age-appropriate kindness, gratitude, care and practical daily examples;
authoritative text quotations and religious claims require review. Respect the
brief's depiction rules. STEAM tasks may use shape patterns, supervised cutting,
folding, paper engineering, unplugged coding and cooperative problem-solving.

## Full-book structure
For 48 pages, allocate exact non-overlapping spans totalling 48 from the brief;
possible sections include ownership/guide, themed units, recap, cut-outs and
assessment. These are options, not fixed pages. Keep already approved pages.
If cut-outs are requested, reserve a non-instructional reverse side and check
physical front/back registration. Never put an active worksheet behind cut-outs.
